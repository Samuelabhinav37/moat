Frequently asked questions

Is Moat free?

Yes. Moat is free and open source under GPL-3.0. There's no paid tier, no premium unlock and no subscription.

What does Moat block?

Ads, trackers, hijacked pop-up tabs and cookie banners, out of the box. Device protection and leaked-password warnings are switches in Settings.

Does Moat collect my data?

No. Blocking runs on your device, and there's no account and no analytics. Once a day it downloads small, signed files of filter fixes and security lists, and those requests carry nothing about you. Two optional switches go online when you turn them on: hidden-tracker detection asks Cloudflare where an address really points, and leaked-password warnings send a short scrambled piece of the password to Have I Been Pwned. Something reaches Moat's developer only when you send it: a problem report, after seeing exactly what it contains, or your answer to the occasional "How is Moat working for you?" question.

How does Moat make money?

It doesn't. Moat is an open-source project that runs very little online, so there's little to pay for. Nothing you do is sold, and it doesn't show "acceptable" ads.

How does Moat work?

Filter lists run inside the browser's own blocking engine, so ads and trackers are stopped before they load. Small signed updates arrive once a day. Read the technical details.

Why does it ask to read every site?

An ad blocker has to see a page's requests to block them, on every site you visit. Moat reads pages on your computer only and never sends their content anywhere.

Can I pause it on one site?

Yes. Click the Moat icon on that site and turn off its switch. Everywhere else stays protected. Kai shows you how.

Does it work in Firefox?

Yes. Moat is built from one codebase for Chrome and Firefox. For now, Firefox users build it from source.

Where do I get it?

From the Chrome Web Store. It's free. You can also build it from source on GitHub.
