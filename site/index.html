How to use Moat.

Most days you won't need to. When you do, it's one click away, and Kai walks you through it.

Hi, I'm Kai. Let me show you around.

Fix a site that breaks

Now and then a site won't load right with ads blocked. You can turn Moat off for just that site.

  1. Click the Moat icon next to the address bar.
  2. Turn off the switch next to the site's name.
  3. The page reloads without Moat.

Every other site stays protected. Turn the switch back on whenever you like.

Hide anything the lists miss

A banner or box the filter lists don't catch? Point at it and it's gone, every time you visit.

  1. Click the Moat icon, then Hide something on this page.
  2. Point at the thing you want gone and click it.
  3. Click Hide on the site's name.

Got too little or too much? Use Select more or Select less. You can bring anything back in Settings.

Get past tracked email links

Many emails send their links through a tracker first. Moat stops the tracker and shows you where the link was going.

  1. Click the link in your email as usual.
  2. Moat stops the tracker page.
  3. Click Go to the site's name to skip it.

Open anyway loads the page once. A site on a scam or phishing list gets a stronger warning instead.

Choose how much Moat blocks

Balanced suits most people. Go Strict for more privacy, or Light if sites keep breaking.

  1. Click the Moat icon, then Settings.
  2. Open Protection.
  3. Pick Light, Balanced or Strict.

Extras like blocking cross-site cookies sit just below. Each one has its own switch.

Tell us when something's off

An ad got through, or a page broke? Send a report to Moat's developer in a few clicks.

  1. Click the Moat icon, then Report a problem.
  2. Pick what's wrong.
  3. Check What will be sent, then click Send report.

You see exactly what gets sent before you send it. No account needed.

Frequently asked questions

Is Moat free?

Yes. Moat is free and open source under GPL-3.0. There's no paid tier, no premium unlock and no subscription.

What does Moat block?

Ads, trackers, hijacked pop-up tabs and cookie banners, out of the box. Device protection and leaked-password warnings are switches in Settings.

Does Moat collect my data?

No. Blocking runs on your device, and there's no account and no analytics. Once a day it downloads small, signed files of filter fixes and security lists, and those requests carry nothing about you. Two optional switches go online when you turn them on: hidden-tracker detection asks Cloudflare where an address really points, and leaked-password warnings send a short scrambled piece of the password to Have I Been Pwned. The only time anything reaches Moat's developer is a problem report you write and send yourself, after seeing exactly what it contains.

How does Moat make money?

It doesn't. Moat is an open-source project that runs very little online, so there's little to pay for. Nothing you do is sold, and it doesn't show "acceptable" ads.

How does Moat work?

Filter lists run inside the browser's own blocking engine, so ads and trackers are stopped before they load. Small signed updates arrive once a day. Read the technical details.

Why does it ask to read every site?

An ad blocker has to see a page's requests to block them, on every site you visit. Moat reads pages on your computer only and never sends their content anywhere.

Can I turn it off for one site?

Yes. Open the Moat popup on that site and flip the switch. Everywhere else stays protected. Kai shows you how.

Does it work in Firefox?

Yes. Moat is built from one codebase for Chrome and Firefox. For now, Firefox users build it from source.

Where do I get it?

From the Chrome Web Store. It's free. You can also build it from source on GitHub.
