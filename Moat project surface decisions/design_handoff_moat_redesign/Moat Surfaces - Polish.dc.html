Turn 12 · About

About is doing four jobs at once

I read the real tab. It's thirteen paragraphs in one card, and they aren't the same kind of thing —

  1. The privacy disclosure is prose. Five paragraphs, each opening with a bold phrase — Filter updates, Breach checking, Settings sync. Turn 9 · List + detail, brought forward

What's actually dated about 8c

I read the piece. Five things in it land directly on our page, and four of them are things 8c gets wrong —

  1. Every module is a visible box. Their point: a card layout means charts treated consistently — same title position, same spacing — not each one wrapped in its own bordered div. Borders on everything is the single biggest tell of a 2010s panel UI. 8c has a border on all eleven rows plus the pane.
  2. Nothing orients you. They open the anatomy with "getting oriented" — a page title and a plain description of what you can do here. 8c drops you straight into switches.
  3. No baselines. "Comparisons and baselines are lacking" is their named failure mode: a number with nothing to compare against is just a number. Moat has real data — blocked counts, sites, per-setting impact — and the settings page shows none of it.
  4. Flat type. They make the case for big, confident numbers: it signals you know which figure matters. Every value in 8c is 12.5px.
  5. The third column is permanent. Their drill-in patterns are the drawer (overlays, keeps context) and the details page. A fixed third column is neither, and it eats width even when you're not reading it.

Both options below keep list + detail, the side rail and the 8d icon. 9a takes the dashboard reading literally; 9b takes the modern-settings reading. One caution I'd raise either way: this is a settings page, not an analytics product — the data earns its place only because it tells you the protections are working.

Turn 8 · Icons

Yes — and it's a bug, not a preference

I pulled the real PNGs out of the repo and looked at them. Three findings, in order of severity —

  1. The mark is white on transparent, with no plate. logo-banner.svg has the dark #030307 tile; the shipped PNGs don't. On Chrome's default light toolbar the icon is white-on-near-white — effectively invisible.
  2. The nodes are sub-pixel at 16. Two dots are r="1.43" on a 32 grid — 0.7px radius when rendered at 16. icon16.png renders as an all-but-blank square.
  3. The glow costs and returns nothing. A feGaussianBlur at 1.4 is a third of the icon at favicon scale; it just greys the edges.

Three directions, each rendered at every size the manifest ships plus a 16px toolbar test on both themes. The 16px row is the one that decides it.

8d Constellation, made to survive

Lowest risk: the same idea, engineered. All four nodes and the branching Y stay — that fork is the silhouette, and dropping it turns the mark into a generic diagonal arrow. What changes is what actually broke: radii go to 4 / 2.8 / 2.2 / 2.0 so nothing is sub-pixel, strokes to 2px, blur deleted, and the #030307 plate from the banner file becomes part of every size. Still the busiest of the three at 16px.
